# Jade Parker

Former U.S. counterterrorism analyst who worked as a contractor for Joint Special Operations Command (JSOC) against ISIS, and spent years warning about "accelerationist" terror networks. In June 2022 she posted that she had "a very good idea" who the unidentified January 6 pipe bomber and the network behind them were. Within days she was gone — her accounts deleted, her online footprint wiped, and she has not been heard from publicly since.

## Assessment: SUSPICIOUS

A counterterrorism analyst with JSOC experience publicly said she believed she had identified the person — and the network — behind the most important unsolved case of January 6, the pipe bombs at the RNC and DNC. She said she had not given the name to anyone yet. Within days she went dark, and nearly every trace of her on the public internet disappeared. Investigative reporter Ken Silva could not reach her, her colleagues did not answer, and the FBI declined to answer questions. There is **no evidence she is dead** and no official missing-person report has surfaced — but the timing, the scrubbing of her online presence, and the stakes of what she said she knew make her silence one of the open mysteries of the pipe bomb case.

## The Claim: "I have a very good idea who the unidentified Jan 6 bomber" is

On **June 8, 2022**, Parker's account @unmoder8d posted a series of tweets. A widely shared screenshot reads:

> "I have a very good idea who the unidentified Jan 6 bomber & affiliated network. I haven't given the name to anyone. I want Upchurch & her red cell to run it down & get full credit in an official professional capacity if I'm right."

"Upchurch" is presumed to be H.E. Upchurch, a researcher who studies neofascist and accelerationist networks.

In the same thread, Parker said her information was "worth $100,000 to the Department of Justice" — a reference to the FBI's $100,000 reward (later raised to $500,000) for information on the bomber — while adding that "none of this is about money." She tied her identification to her years of research mapping accelerationist networks.

On **June 9, 2022**, she followed up, asking whether her followers had seen the part about using her research to identify the person who planted the DNC and RNC bombs — "a super interesting one if I'm right."

That is the last known public activity from her.

## The Disappearance

After June 9, 2022, Parker "seemingly vanished," according to Headline USA. Almost all traces of her disappeared from the publicly available internet. What survives:

- **Deleted tweets** archived on the Wayback Machine, and screenshots circulated by researchers
- **A Google Drive document** she posted and referred to as a "dead man switch"
- **Earlier public work** — her 2020 GNET article on accelerationism, her Lawfare contributor page, a 2018 VOA quote, and her 2021 *Team House* podcast interview

When Ken Silva of Headline USA investigated in October 2023 — 16 months after the tweets — Parker could not be reached for comment, her colleagues did not respond to emails, and the FBI declined to answer questions about its pipe bomb investigation. As of October 2026, no news outlet has reported making contact with her, and no official body has said where she is.

### The "Dead Man Switch" Document

The Google Drive document Parker left online is not the smoking gun many expected. According to Headline USA and the Libertarian Institute:

- It centers on her claim that academics **stole or misused her research** on accelerationism
- It contains a detailed analysis of the **Order of the Nine Angles (O9A)**, a Satanic neo-Nazi network
- It mentions January 6 only in passing and does **not** name a pipe bomber

Parker wrote that the "strategic intent of O9A is to symbolically destroy the Holy Roman Empire, represented by the United States."

Some researchers read the document as the background for her identification — a map of the network she believed was involved — rather than the identification itself.

## Background

### JSOC Counterterrorism Work

Parker was described on *The Team House* podcast (hosted by former Green Beret Jack Murphy, July 31, 2021) as having "served as a contractor working for JSOC conducting cyber ops against ISIS." The episode was titled "Cyber Special Reconnaissance." Headline USA described her as a "credentialed terrorism analyst" who had worked with numerous government agencies. In 2018, Voice of America quoted her as a former counterterrorism analyst in support of U.S. military activities, commenting on ISIS threats to Manbij, Syria.

### Terrorism Research

- She was listed by **Lawfare** as a Senior Research Associate at the **Terror Asymmetrics Project (TAPSTRI)**. According to researcher BX (@bx_on_x), TAPSTRI was Malcolm Nance's project.
- In February 2020 she published **"Accelerationism in America: Threat Perceptions"** for the Global Network on Extremism and Technology (GNET). She defined accelerationism as an "ideologically agnostic doctrine" of violent and non-violent action meant to speed the collapse of a political system by exploiting its contradictions. She described "accelerationist terrorism as theater" — attacks designed to polarize the public — and warned of rising political violence before the 2020 election. She argued that accelerationists from fascist, socialist, and anarchist circles cooperate across ideologies online and recruit inside niche communities.
- She studied the **Order of the Nine Angles (O9A)**. Reporting on O9A has linked it to several neo-Nazi terror plots, and a member of the network, Joshua Caleb Sutter, was revealed to be a longtime FBI informant. The Libertarian Institute reports that O9A is allegedly led by David Myatt, who it describes as tied to Cold War stay-behind networks; Myatt has denied founding or leading O9A.

### What She Said About January 6

On *The Team House* in July 2021, Parker said she had spent years trying to warn the U.S. intelligence community about accelerationism and that nobody listened. She argued that it was wrong to label the January 6 crowd itself an accelerationist threat. Her view was that accelerationists **exploited** the crowd — using it to deepen divisions and to make Americans believe a civil war was coming. "Because if you get the myth, you can mobilize people," she said.

That is the frame her June 2022 claim came out of: not that ordinary protesters planted the bombs, but that a network working to provoke national conflict did.

## The Pipe Bomb Case: Why Her Claim Matters

On the evening of **January 5, 2021**, between roughly 7:30 and 8:30 p.m., a hooded, masked person placed two pipe bombs — one outside the Republican National Committee headquarters and one outside the Democratic National Committee headquarters in Washington, D.C. They were not found until about 1 p.m. on January 6, around the time the Capitol was breached. Vice President-elect Kamala Harris was inside the DNC when that bomb was found.

For nearly five years, the FBI did not name a suspect. Questions mounted:

- **Sen. Ron Johnson** called it "odd" that Secret Service sweeps and bomb dogs did not detect the DNC device before Harris's visit.
- **Rep. Thomas Massie** pressed FBI Director Christopher Wray at a July 2023 hearing: "It was 900 days ago when this happened... You need to tell us what you found." Wray did not give answers.
- **Kyle Seraphin**, a suspended FBI agent turned whistleblower, told the *Washington Times* that investigators had traced the suspect to a Metro SmarTrip card bought by a retired Air Force chief master sergeant who was a cleared security contractor, that the FBI had a license plate from Metro footage, and that the devices could not detonate. On a podcast, he said the suspect "is or was a federal source" for some agency. These are Seraphin's claims and have not been confirmed by the FBI.
- **Steven D'Antuono**, former head of the FBI's Washington Field Office, disputed Seraphin in a June 2023 House Judiciary interview, saying the FBI lab found the devices viable. He acknowledged that one provider's cell phone geofence data had been "corrupted."

Parker's tweets came in June 2022 — at a time when the FBI said it still had no suspect.

### The Arrest of Brian Cole Jr. — and the Dispute

On **December 4, 2025**, the FBI arrested **Brian Cole Jr.** and charged him with transporting and planting the two devices. According to court filings, Cole first denied involvement and then confessed about two hours into an FBI interview after agents showed him a still image from surveillance video; prosecutors also cite cell tower records placing his phone near the RNC and DNC. He has since been indicted on federal charges — superseding indictments in 2026 added terrorism-related counts that carry a possible life sentence. **Cole has pleaded not guilty**, has been held without bail, and his trial has been set for February 16. A federal judge rejected his lawyers' argument that the January 6 pardons cover his conduct. Cole has not been convicted of any crime.

The case against him is disputed:

- In September 2026, Cole's parents told Tucker Carlson they believe their son was framed.
- On **October 17–18, 2026**, independent reporter **Steve Baker** released never-before-seen Capitol CCTV footage (video work by @accabbat, "Armitas") that he says shows Cole walking on the **northwest** side of the Capitol at the same moment the real suspect was placing devices on the **southeast** side. Baker said Cole's grandmother told him the jacket in the footage, with a tag still on it, was a recent Christmas gift. Baker and Kyle Seraphin broke the footage down together on video.
- Rep. Thomas Massie, Marjorie Taylor Greene, Michael Flynn, and FBI whistleblowers Steve Friend and Kyle Seraphin publicly backed Baker's report. Greene called for Cole to be freed.
- The FBI and DOJ have not confirmed that the man in Baker's video is Cole, and did not immediately respond to requests for comment. How Baker obtained the footage has not been disclosed.

As this dispute grew, X users resurfaced Parker's story. BX (@bx_on_x), whose #WhereIsJade thread ranks it first among their original research, wrote that Parker "figured out who the pipe bomber was and went missing." Other posts argue that if the wrong man is in custody, the person she said she had identified — and the "affiliated network" — may never have been charged.

**We do not know who Parker suspected.** She never published the name. BX has said they also do not know whom she suspected or whether her assessment was right — only that she pursued the truth on her own and that, in BX's view, significant effort appeared to be made to silence her.

## Intelligence Connections

- Former JSOC contractor running cyber operations against ISIS — she had direct experience with the U.S. special operations and intelligence world
- Lawfare-listed Senior Research Associate at TAPSTRI (Terror Asymmetrics Project), a counterterrorism research project
- Her research focused on O9A, a network that included at least one longtime FBI informant (Joshua Caleb Sutter)
- Her claim concerned an FBI investigation that went unsolved for nearly five years, about which an FBI whistleblower has alleged (without FBI confirmation) that the suspect "is or was a federal source"
- The FBI declined to answer questions from Headline USA about her claim

## Why This Disappearance Raises Questions

- **Timing:** She went silent within about a day of saying she knew who the bomber was — while the FBI said it had no suspect and was offering a reward.
- **She hadn't told anyone the name.** By her own words, the information was in her head alone, which would make her the only source.
- **Her footprint was wiped.** Nearly all of her public online presence disappeared at once. Only archived copies remain.
- **She posted a "dead man switch."** People who post dead man switches generally do so because they fear for their safety.
- **She had the skills.** This was not an anonymous internet sleuth. She was a JSOC-trained cyber analyst whose job had been mapping terror networks online.
- **Nobody official has said where she is.** No police statement, no family statement, no welfare-check report has become public in more than four years.
- **The case she spoke about is still contested.** The man charged in 2025 has pleaded not guilty, and new video released in October 2026 is being cited by members of Congress as evidence he could not have been the bomber.

## Counterarguments / Alternative Explanations

- **There is no evidence she is dead or was harmed.** No missing-person report, no family appeal, and no law-enforcement statement has been found. "Vanished from the internet" is not the same as "vanished."
- **She may have chosen to go private.** Deleting accounts after a heated public thread is common, especially for people with security-cleared or contractor careers who may have been warned against public statements.
- **The "dead man switch" did not name a bomber.** The document was mostly about her dispute with academics over her research, which some readers see as personal grievance rather than a case file.
- **Some online commentators have questioned her background or suggested personal problems.** These claims are anecdotal and unverified, as are the claims that she was silenced.
- **Her identification may simply have been wrong.** She wrote "if I'm right," and never published her reasoning.
- **The FBI has charged someone.** Prosecutors say Brian Cole Jr. confessed and that phone data places him near both sites. If that case holds up at trial, her theory of a wider network may not.
