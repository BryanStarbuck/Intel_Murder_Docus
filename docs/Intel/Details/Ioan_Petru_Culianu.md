# Ioan Petru Culianu
Romanian-born historian of religion at the University of Chicago who studied magic, ecstatic "otherworld" journeys, and the paranormal, and wrote scathing attacks on Romania's post-communist regime and its secret police. Shot once in the back of the head inside a Divinity School restroom in 1991. The killer has never been identified.

## Assessment: SUSPICIOUS

Culianu was killed by a single, precise shot to the back of the head in the middle of a weekday on a university campus, with nothing stolen — circumstances that investigators and later writers have said suggested a professional killing. He had spent the previous year publishing biting attacks on the Securitate and the post-1989 Iliescu government in the Romanian exile press, and reportedly received threatening phone calls shortly before his death. Journalist Ted Anton, who spent years on the case, called it what Culianu's friends had suspected from the start: possibly the first political assassination of a professor on American soil. No suspect has ever been charged, and Romanian intelligence has denied any involvement.

## Circumstances of Death

Shortly after noon on Tuesday, May 21, 1991, Culianu walked from his office to a men's restroom in Swift Hall, the home of the University of Chicago Divinity School. He was shot once in the back of the head with a .25-caliber automatic and left in a stall. He was found around 1 p.m.

Nothing was taken from him. He had no known personal enemies on campus, was popular with students, and was engaged to Hillary Wiesner, a 27-year-old Harvard graduate student. He was weeks away from a permanent appointment at the university, scheduled for July 1991.

Chicago police had no early leads, and the FBI joined the investigation because of the possible foreign-intelligence dimension. The case was eventually closed without an arrest. Records later released to the family and to filmmakers under freedom-of-information requests were, according to scholar Sorin Antohi, heavily redacted.

## Background

Culianu was born in Iași to a family of academics. He graduated from the University of Bucharest in 1972 and, while on a trip to Italy, sought and received political asylum, never returning to live under the Ceaușescu regime. He earned a doctorate in the history of religions in Milan (1975), taught at the University of Groningen in the Netherlands (1976–1985), earned a second doctorate at the Sorbonne (Paris IV, 1987), and joined the University of Chicago in 1986.

He was the protégé, close friend, and later literary executor of **Mircea Eliade**, the most famous historian of religion of the 20th century. Their relationship cooled when Culianu began confronting Eliade's interwar ties to the fascist Iron Guard.

His scholarship centered on what most academics avoided: Renaissance magic, Gnosticism, ecstatic "otherworld" journeys, and the mind's capacity for manipulation. His best-known books include:

* *Eros and Magic in the Renaissance* (1984; English 1987) — argued that Renaissance magicians like Giordano Bruno were early theorists of mass psychological manipulation, a "magic" Culianu saw reborn in modern propaganda and intelligence work.
* *Out of This World: Otherworldly Journeys from Gilgamesh to Albert Einstein* (1991) — a survey of ascent, out-of-body and visionary experiences across cultures, which moves toward modern ideas of higher dimensions.
* *The Tree of Gnosis* (1992, posthumous).

He also wrote fiction, much of it built around hidden-dimension and secret-society themes.

## Intelligence Connections

* **Securitate and its successors.** After the December 1989 revolution, Culianu wrote a series of sharp political essays and satirical stories in the Romanian exile press attacking the new National Salvation Front government of Ion Iliescu and the secret police, which he called a force "of epochal stupidity." The most widely circulated theory is that former Securitate agents — or networks that survived into the new Romanian Intelligence Service (SRI) — carried out or arranged the killing. Wikipedia notes that several pages of his Securitate file are reported missing.
* **Far-right fronts.** Some factions of the Romanian far right openly celebrated his death. Historians including Vladimir Tismăneanu have described Culianu's political writing as one of the most devastating indictments of the alliance between Romania's former communist apparatus and its revived ultranationalists. A recurring theory holds that intelligence networks used legionnaire/Iron Guard émigré circles as cover.
* **Threats.** According to Anton's reporting and later accounts, Culianu received anonymous threatening phone calls in the period before his murder.
* **Denials.** Scholar Moshe Idel, a friend of Culianu, told Radio Romania International that Romanian intelligence either refuses to discuss the case or states simply that "we had nothing to do with Culianu's assassination." Idel also said former Romanian president Emil Constantinescu told him there was "nothing he could do" about the matter.
* **Alternative theory.** In *Secrets, Lies, and Consequences*, historian of religion Bruce Lincoln proposed a different hypothesis tied to Eliade's inner circle, including Eliade's widow Christinel Eliade (died 2002). Idel has said there is no proof for Lincoln's theory.

## Why This Death Raises Questions

- A single, accurate shot to the back of the head — the signature of a professional, not a robbery or a crime of passion.
- Nothing was stolen.
- The killing came about a year after Culianu began publishing open attacks on the post-communist regime and its security services.
- Reported anonymous threats in the days and weeks before he was killed.
- Missing pages in his Securitate file.
- The FBI took an interest because of the foreign-intelligence angle — and still closed the case with no arrest.
- Released government records were heavily redacted, according to people who requested them.
- Parts of the Romanian far right publicly celebrated his death.
- A murder inside a university building, in daylight, by a killer who walked away unseen.

## Counterarguments / Alternative Explanations

- No physical evidence, witness, or document has ever tied the killing to the Securitate, the SRI, or any intelligence service. The intelligence theory rests on motive and method.
- Romanian intelligence has denied involvement.
- Investigators also considered personal and occult-milieu motives: Culianu was in contact with people in esoteric and fringe circles through his research, and some accounts mention a possible link to a member of a fanatical sect.
- Bruce Lincoln's Eliade-circle hypothesis, while unproven, shows that serious scholars do not all accept the political-assassination theory.
