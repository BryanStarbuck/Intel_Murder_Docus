## Assessment: SUSPICIOUS

Janet Ossebaard died under circumstances that combine several patterns seen across this investigation: a researcher who publicly exposed elite trafficking networks, who stated she would never commit suicide, who was found dead weeks later with the official ruling of suicide. Her partner Cyntha Koeter — a former police detective who was closest to her — confirmed the suicide and explicitly denied foul play, stating "No, Janet is not killed by the Cabal or anyone else." However, the five-week delay in discovering her body due to repeated police inaction, her prior public statements about never taking her own life, and the subject matter of her documentaries raise questions that her followers consider significant.

On X, the death is still treated as an assassination. A December 30, 2023 post by @Starboy2079 calling her "clintoned" drew more than 4,200 likes and 521,000 views. An August 21, 2026 memorial by @TheShadowIntelX — the post that prompted this page expansion — drew more than 6,500 likes and 70,000 views in a single day, with reply after reply insisting she did not kill herself. ## Circumstances of Death

On November 16, 2023, Ossebaard left the residence she shared with her partner and collaborator Cyntha Koeter. According to Koeter, Ossebaard departed in a suicidal state, driving away in a camper van. She left without money, personal possessions, or her dog. She left a message indicating she intended to take her own life. Her final Telegram letter to followers contained language consistent with suicidal ideation.

Ossebaard drove to Lund, in southern Sweden, where she parked the camper van on a private property. On November 17, 2023 — one day after her arrival — the property owner called Swedish police to have the van removed. Police did not respond.

In late November 2023, the alternative media outlet Stop World Control — with Koeter's approval — published a missing persons report stating: "For quite some time Janet was not doing well, and she left a few weeks ago with the message that she was going to take her own life."

Approximately two weeks after the first call, the property owner contacted police again. Despite a notification already existing in their system, police still did not respond.

On December 22, 2023, after the property owner called police a third time and Ossebaard's family filed an official missing person report, police finally connected the reports, came to the property, broke open the camper van window, and discovered Ossebaard's body. She had been dead for approximately five weeks. Swedish police in Lund confirmed the cause of death as a medication overdose and ruled it suicide. According to the January 21, 2024 reconstruction by *Algemeen Dagblad* / *De Stentor* reporters Niek Megens and Phaedra Werkhoven, there were no traces of violence. Wikipedia, citing Lund police, states they believe she ended her life on or about the day she went missing — November 16, 2023.

Koeter later criticized the police response, noting their failure to act on the property owner's earlier calls had left the body undiscovered for over a month.

## Background

### Early career

Janet Ossebaard studied English language and literature at the University of Groningen. At age 24, she started her own company, "Ossebaard & Janssen — Training and Consulting Group," providing communication training. She ran the business for approximately 10 years before selling it. She also trained as a natural medicine therapist, worked as a freelance coach and trainer, and according to folklorist Theo Meder (2006) ran a webshop selling crop-circle paraphernalia.

### Crop circle research (1994–2019)

In 1994, Ossebaard encountered a crop circle near the village of Witten in the Netherlands, which she described as a life-changing experience. She told *De Stentor* in 2017 that she had not been looking for a "great shift," that she had been emotionally stable and happy, and that the formation made her "completely still inside." She said she was "not easily impressed" but could not explain what she saw, and that she remained "an analytical animal."

She became a board member, and in 2005 chair, of the Dutch Centre for Crop Circle Studies. From 1995 she spent every summer in southern England mapping formations and taking soil samples. She said she had personally witnessed a circle being formed three times. She believed crop circles were communications from extraterrestrial beings. She built a devoted following on the Dutch alternative circuit.

Theo Meder, professor of folk narrative, joined DCCCS in the early 2000s to study the group's stories. In a 2020 Pointer interview he described the members as orderly, somewhat dull people with an unshakable belief that circles were made by extraterrestrial or supernatural forces — and, stacked on top of that, a catalogue of Illuminati / New World Order theories. Paranoia about being watched by the Dutch security service (AIVD) was common; Meder's note-taking led some members to decide he must be an agent. He described Ossebaard as among the more eccentric members. After he published research concluding the circles were not extraterrestrial, Ossebaard — by then chair — expelled him by email as persona non grata.

Between 2015 and 2018 she chaired the Graancirkel Centrum in Deventer and briefly operated a small crop-circle museum there. She appeared in the documentary *Crop Circles: Quest for Truth* (2002). A 2019 VPRO short, *Een boodschap van buitenaf* (Ramon Hoogerhuis and Maaike Ruigrok), filmed her in a grey fur coat with short red-dyed hair, binoculars around her neck and a high-powered laser pen in her pocket, sitting in a field with her white Labrador Claudy, signaling alleged craft. "And guaranteed they answer. Bang! With a flash. Sometimes three times." Pointer reported there was "not a grain of irony in her voice."

She was also, according to Conspiracy Watch and Pepijn van Erp, a long-time supporter of Dutch psychic Robbert van den Broeke, who has been shown to use photographic tricks. Van den Broeke claimed to channel, among others, assassinated Dutch politician Pim Fortuyn. After Ossebaard's death, Conspiracy Watch reported, van den Broeke claimed he had already been in contact with her in the afterlife, that she was happy, and that there are crop circles in that other world too. Van den Broeke is alive; those claims are his, reported by Conspiracy Watch.

Dutch Wikipedia also notes she published on whether the beings in Tolkien were fiction or reality (*Frontier Magazine*, 2005).

## "Fall of the Cabal" (2019–2023)

In 2019, Ossebaard completed a 10-part documentary series titled "Fall of the Cabal" (*De Val van de Cabal*). Initially screened in small venues and sold on DVD, the series was released free on YouTube in early 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic, which brought massive international attention. According to Conspiracy Watch, the series was translated into 25 languages and its total runtime exceeded 10 hours.

Pepijn van Erp, who published a part-by-part fact-check in 2020, recorded the title card of Part 1 as **"The End of the World As We Know It – Things That Make You Go Hmmmm…"** In the pre-title text she urged viewers to accept nothing as truth, do their own research, and double-check everything she presented.

The documentary covered topics including:

- Alleged elite child trafficking networks and pedophilia
- Jeffrey Epstein and his island
- QAnon theories and "Q drops"
- Shadow governments and the "deep state"
- Banking power structures (she named the Rothschilds and Rockefellers; those are her claims, not court findings)
- Historical conspiracy theories
- Central banks, media, and what she called satanic networks at the highest levels (as later summarized on X)

According to Conspiracy Watch, the series became "the most popular and influential QAnon documentary." Sociologist J.M. Wright (Cambridge University Press, 2023) called it a general introduction to the "QAnon religion." A 2020 *CTC Sentinel* paper by Amarnath Amarasingam and Marc-André Argentino cited its influence on that community. Argentino (2023) described it as liturgical material inside QAnon. Dutch Wikipedia, citing Meder and Manuvie, calls her a key figure in spreading QAnon ideas in the Low Countries at the start of the COVID crisis. Criminologist Fiore Geelhoed described her mix as QAnon child-abuse claims stacked with aliens, chemtrails, vaccines, and 5G.

It was removed from YouTube and migrated to alternative platforms including Rumble and Bitchute. After platform restrictions she shut the YouTube channel; communication with followers moved to Telegram. The official site is [fallcabal.com](https://www.fallcabal.com/). In 2023, Conspiracy Watch reported, US right-wing influencer Dom Lucre (Dominick McGee, over a million X followers) used X's long-video feature to upload the complete first series; CW said it was still there in January 2024. Lucre is alive; that is a report of a republication, not an endorsement of the content.

## Nomadic lifestyle and pressures

After gaining prominence, Ossebaard and Koeter adopted a nomadic lifestyle, frequently relocating. Some followers attributed this to safety concerns related to the content of their documentaries. Conspiracy Watch wrote that they spent most of their time in Spain and expressed discontent with what they called the "dictatorship" of the Netherlands. In June 2021, both Ossebaard and Koeter were reported missing for three days, alarming their followers, before they resurfaced without explanation.

According to Koeter, in the months before her death, Ossebaard suffered from "severe emotional weariness" caused by the intensive pace of documentary production and a "forced move" (relocation) that triggered a downward spiral. Koeter stated that Ossebaard had struggled with severe depression and "death wishes" throughout her life, and that this was not a new condition.

- **Prior statements about never committing suicide:** Multiple sources report that Ossebaard stated publicly over the preceding years that she would never take her own life. - **Five-week delay in body discovery:** Swedish police failed to respond to the property owner's calls on November 17 and again approximately two weeks later, despite having a notification in their system. The body was not found until December 22, 2023.
- **Pattern of researchers dying:** Ossebaard's death fits a broader pattern documented in this investigation of individuals who publicly accused elite networks of trafficking and abuse dying under contested circumstances — including [Sabrina Bittencourt](Sabrina_Bittencourt.md), [Natacha Jaitt](Natacha_Jaitt.md), [Isaac Kappy](/intelligence-service-murders/Details/Isaac_Kappy/), and [Tracy Twyman](Tracy_Twyman.md).

- **Immediate "Clintoned" framing on X:** Within days of the body being found, high-reach posts (notably @Starboy2079, 4,288 likes, 521,218 views) claimed Google had erased her and that she had been murdered in the style of the "Clinton body count" myth she herself had promoted. - **Her partner confirmed it was suicide.** Cyntha Koeter — a former police detective and the person closest to Ossebaard — explicitly stated: "No, Janet is not killed by the Cabal or anyone else." She described Ossebaard as "just a human being as we all are, with her own troubles of heart and mind."
- **Koeter went further than a general denial.** According to Dutch Wikipedia, citing fallcabal.com, Koeter specifically denied a UFO beam-up, protection by "Q," murder by "the Cabal," murder by Koeter herself, and the claim that Ossebaard had secretly started a new life.
- **Lifelong mental health struggles.** According to Koeter, Ossebaard suffered from severe depression, anxiety disorders, and "death wishes" throughout her life. This was not a sudden or unexplained decline.
- **Childhood trauma.** Koeter indicated Ossebaard experienced childhood trauma that contributed to her mental health challenges. *AD* reported her last letters mused about a dominant mother.
- **Clear suicide indicators.** Ossebaard left a suicide message before departing. She left without money, possessions, or her dog — consistent with someone not planning to return. The November 5 letter said she did not think followers would hear from her again and closed "until next time, friends."
- **Emotional deterioration documented in her own posts.** A forced move and the intensive pace of documentary work triggered a downward spiral. *AD* and *De Groene* describe months of "transition," a break with Cabal theory, 10 kilograms of weight loss, and selling everything.
- **Cause of death consistent with suicide.** Medication overdose in a private setting is consistent with the suicide ruling. *AD* reported no traces of violence.
- **Police conclusion.** Lund police ruled suicide and, per Wikipedia, believed she died on or about November 16, 2023 — the day she left.
- **The "never commit suicide" pattern.** While Ossebaard reportedly made such statements, her final communications contradicted them. People who previously stated they would never take their own lives can and do die by suicide — particularly those with documented histories of depression.
- **The "erased from Google" claim is false.** Dutch national newspapers (*AD*, *De Stentor*, *De Telegraaf*, NOS, *Trouw*, *De Groene Amsterdammer*, ND), Wikipedia, Pointer, and Conspiracy Watch all covered her in her own name. The December 2023 X claim that "everything about her has been omitted from the net" does not match the public record.
