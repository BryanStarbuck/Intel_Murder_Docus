## Assessment: SUSPICIOUS

Jenny Moore was not a random conspiracy theorist. She was a credentialed law enforcement veteran — a retired corporal from the Tracy, California Police Department with 11 years of service, including narcotics investigations and work as a Field Training Officer. She held a Master's degree in Psychology. She had been conducting documented child trafficking investigations for years, and in the summer of 2017 was physically attacked by four assailants outside her home in San Jose, California — an attack she reported to police but said was suspiciously under-investigated.

Six weeks before her death, she had submitted detailed allegations of child sexual abuse by a high-profile political figure to the Department of Homeland Security (July 6-9, 2018) and the FBI (approximately July 13, 2018). She then conducted a recorded final interview on July 13th describing the victim and the allegations in detail — an interview that circulated widely after her death. On August 13, 2018, she was found dead in a Maryland hotel room of an apparent seizure. She was 49 years old. No toxicology results were publicly released, no final cause of death was officially confirmed, and police said it was not a suicide and not a homicide — leaving the actual cause of death officially unresolved.

The combination of a prior violent attack, documented physical threats, recent submission of explosive allegations to federal law enforcement, a recorded final interview expressing awareness of personal danger, and sudden death of unresolved cause in a Washington D.C.-area hotel room constitute a pattern serious enough to document.

## Circumstances of Death

On August 13, 2018, hotel staff at the Country Inn & Suites by Radisson in Capitol Heights, Maryland — a suburb of Washington D.C. — found Jenny Moore unresponsive in her room. Emergency services were called. She was pronounced dead.

A Prince George's County Police spokesperson told reporters the death did not appear to be a suicide and that police found nothing suspicious about her death. The cause was described as an apparent seizure.

Crucially: at the time of initial reporting, toxicology results had not yet returned. Police stated the toxicology results — which would determine the actual cause of death — were not due for approximately one month. Moore's body was sent to the Maryland state coroner's office in Baltimore and then to the state anatomy lab for autopsy.

No final toxicology or autopsy results were ever publicly released or reported. The cause of death — "apparent seizure" — remained a preliminary description, not a confirmed medical finding.

Moore had reportedly traveled to Washington D.C. specifically to follow up on the child abuse allegations she had submitted to federal authorities. She was not known to associates as having any preexisting seizure disorder, cardiac condition, or other illness that would explain sudden death.

## Background

### Law Enforcement Career

Jenny Moore served the Tracy, California Police Department from 1993 until her retirement in 2004 — 11 years of service. She held the rank of Corporal. She worked in the Street Crime Unit as a narcotics investigator. She served as a DARE officer in the Tracy Unified School District. She served as a Field Training Officer, teaching new officers how to police. According to the Tracy Police Department's own public announcement of her death: "As a Field Training Officer, Jenny taught new officers how to police in Tracy and gave them an appreciation for the oath they took."

After retirement from law enforcement, Moore returned to school. She earned a Master's degree in Psychology, completing her graduate degree in 2009-2010. She subsequently worked with abused and traumatized children.

### Investigative Journalism

Using the online alias "Task Force," Moore collaborated with investigative journalist George Webb — who maintains a YouTube channel focused on government corruption and trafficking networks. According to accounts from that period, Moore provided extensive research support to Webb's investigations on weekdays, while on weekends she conducted direct interviews with trafficking victims and abuse survivors.

Moore had previously reported on Operation Yewtree, the British Metropolitan Police investigation into historical child sexual abuse by UK celebrities — the investigation that exposed Jimmy Savile and led to the conviction of multiple high-profile British figures.

By 2018, Moore had shifted her focus to alleged child sexual abuse by American political figures. She was working with a 26-year-old man who claimed he had been sexually abused as a child and "pimped out" at private parties attended by Washington D.C. elites.

### The 2017 Physical Attack

In the summer of 2017 — approximately a year before her death — Moore was attacked outside her home in a gated community in San Jose, California. Four to five unknown assailants attacked her. Although she fought back, she sustained multiple injuries, including significant head wounds.

Moore reported the attack to San Jose police. According to accounts from people who knew her, she stated the investigation by San Jose police "seemed suspiciously weak." She suffered serious injuries. Associates who knew Moore in the months following the attack believed the head wounds she sustained may have contributed to her eventual health decline — though this remains unconfirmed.

The attack is documented in independent media accounts from that period. It has not been publicly connected to her trafficking investigations by law enforcement, but the timing — one year before her death, during the period she was conducting her most sensitive investigations — is noted.

## What She Was Investigating and Reporting to Federal Authorities

According to multiple accounts reported in the Washington Standard, Speroforum, and other outlets, Moore spent 2017-2018 working with a then-26-year-old man (whose identity was not publicly disclosed) who alleged that as a child he had been sexually assaulted by a prominent political figure and exploited at private sex parties attended by Washington D.C. power elites. The name of the political figure alleged by the victim was reported by alternative media outlets and attributed to the victim's own testimony — these are allegations, not established facts.

Between July 6 and July 9, 2018, Moore submitted documentation of the alleged victim's claims to the Department of Homeland Security. One week later, on approximately July 13, 2018, she submitted identical documentation to the FBI.

Also on July 13, Moore gave a recorded interview to YouTuber and radio journalist "Farmer Jones," describing in detail what the alleged victim had told her. This interview circulated widely after her death and was described by those who saw it as containing "chilling details of abuse." Moore, according to associates, accepted the risks and was told things could "get very ugly very fast." Her colleague described her as "fearless" and "an absolute warrior" who "I don't think was afraid of anyone or anything."

Six weeks after filing with the FBI, Moore was dead.

The FBI, citing standard policy, neither confirmed nor denied receiving any submissions from Moore.

## Why This Death Raises Questions

- **Documented prior physical attack:** A year before her death, four to five assailants attacked Moore outside her home in San Jose. She reported the attack but said the investigation was suspiciously weak. She sustained head injuries.
- **Active threats on record:** Moore had reported threats to associates and stated she had been targeted through online harassment. She accepted that the investigation she was pursuing carried serious personal risk.
- **Six-week timeline from FBI submission to death:** Moore submitted explosive allegations to DHS on July 6-9, 2018, and to the FBI on approximately July 13. She was dead on August 13 — exactly five weeks after the FBI submission.
- **No known seizure history:** At age 49, with no publicly documented seizure disorder or cardiac condition, a sudden seizure death is medically notable. Seizure can be induced by various means including certain drug combinations that can evade standard toxicology panels depending on timing and detection protocols.
- **Toxicology results never released:** The initial reports explicitly noted that toxicology results were not yet available. No subsequent reporting or official release of those results has been identified. The actual confirmed cause of death remains publicly unknown.
- **Hotel room, far from home:** Moore died in a Washington D.C.-area hotel room — she was not at her California home. She had come to the D.C. area specifically in connection with her federal agency submissions and follow-up.
- **Police ruling of "not suspicious" made before toxicology returned:** The Prince George's County Police determination that the death was not suspicious was made before toxicology and autopsy results were available — meaning the ruling was based on scene investigation alone, not confirmed cause of death.
- **Final interview**: Moore gave a detailed interview five weeks before her death laying out explosive allegations. Her awareness that this work could get her killed is documented on tape.
- **Pattern:** Moore's death occurred during a period when multiple individuals investigating elite child sex abuse — [Nancy Schaefer](Nancy_Schaefer.mdx), [Monica Petersen](Monica_Petersen.mdx), [Tracy Twyman](Tracy_Twyman.mdx) — died under contested circumstances within a few years of each other.

## The Counterargument

The Prince George's County Police investigated the scene and found nothing suspicious. Snopes rated the foul-play claims as "unproven." The Daily Beast characterized Moore as a Pizzagate-adjacent conspiracy theorist rather than a serious investigator, noting her promotion of unverified claims.

A seizure death in a 49-year-old woman is medically possible with no prior diagnosed history — many seizure disorders are undiagnosed, and cardiac arrhythmia can present similarly. The head injuries from her 2017 attack could, theoretically, have contributed to neurological vulnerability. There is no physical evidence from the scene indicating foul play. The FBI's refusal to confirm her submissions is standard policy, not unusual.

It is possible that Jenny Moore, a physically active woman who had sustained significant head trauma in 2017 and was under extreme stress conducting sensitive investigations, suffered a genuine medical episode. The absence of toxicology results from public record does not necessarily indicate concealment — results are often not publicly released unless there is a criminal proceeding.

On August 19, 2018 — six days after her death — another post circulated: *"Prior To Her Untimely Death Last Week: More Info About How Jenny Moore 'Task Force' Was Threatened & Told To Keep her Mouth Shut About Victims Of #ChildTrafficking #Corruption & #ChildSexAbuse."* This echoed accounts from Moore's associates that she had been warned prior to her death.

Some X users framed Moore's death within a broader narrative of claimed suspicious deaths around political figures — a contested framing with no forensic support in Moore's case specifically. Her work with George Webb was also cited in X discussions; some threads referenced Moore's investigations into trafficking networks reportedly tied to Racine, Wisconsin, with one post claiming "the Racine thing died with Task Force" — suggesting her death halted investigative threads she had been pursuing independently. These Racine-related claims have not been verified by any independent or mainstream source. Additional posts referenced her alleged role in filing legal complaints against government figures and broader allegations involving satanic ritual abuse in elite trafficking networks; these claims originate in online communities and carry no documented evidentiary support.

The date discrepancy in the posts — some citing August 12, others August 13 — reflects confusion between when Moore was found unresponsive and when death was officially recorded; the Prince George's County Police and mainstream accounts settled on August 13.
