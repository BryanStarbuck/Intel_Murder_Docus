## Assessment: SUSPICIOUS

Diana predicted her own murder in writing, naming a specific method — a staged car crash with brake failure causing a head injury — in a note she gave to her attorney Lord Mishcon in October 1995, and in a separate letter to her butler Paul Burrell in 1996. That note was locked in a police safe for six years before being handed to investigators. The driver Henri Paul's blood alcohol results were disputed by pathologists hired by the Al-Fayed family, who noted colleagues described him as appearing completely sober that night and that blood sample contamination questions were never resolved. A former MI6 officer, Richard Tomlinson, signed a sworn statement alleging intelligence service involvement — though he later qualified the statement. France sealed its 6,000-page investigation dossier until 2082. The British inquest jury verdict was "unlawful killing" — not accident. ## Circumstances of Death

### The Night of August 30–31, 1997

Diana, Princess of Wales, and her companion Dodi Fayed spent the evening of August 30, 1997, in Paris. After dining at the Ritz Hotel — owned by Dodi's father Mohamed Al-Fayed — they prepared to leave for Dodi's apartment. Anticipating pursuit by paparazzi, the security team arranged for a decoy car to leave from the front entrance while Diana and Dodi departed from a rear exit.

Their car — a black Mercedes-Benz S280 — was driven by Henri Paul, the Ritz's deputy head of security, who had not originally been scheduled to drive that night. Trevor Rees-Jones, Diana's personal bodyguard, occupied the front passenger seat.

At approximately 12:23 a.m. on August 31, 1997, the Mercedes entered the Pont de l'Alma tunnel at high speed while being pursued by photographers on motorcycles. Inside the tunnel, the car struck the right-side wall, then collided with a concrete pillar and spun across the road.

Dodi Fayed and Henri Paul were killed instantly. Diana was alive but critically injured. She was removed from the wreckage and transported by ambulance to Pitié-Salpêtrière Hospital, where doctors fought to save her for approximately two hours before pronouncing her dead at 4:00 a.m.

Trevor Rees-Jones was the sole survivor. He had been wearing a seatbelt. Diana, Dodi, and Henri Paul had not.

### The Official Finding

The 2008 British inquest jury, sitting under Lord Justice Scott Baker after six months of testimony, returned a majority verdict (9–2) of **unlawful killing** caused by "the grossly negligent driving of the following vehicles [paparazzi] and of the Mercedes driver Henri Paul." The jury explicitly did not find that the crash was an accident. They found it was an unlawful killing — the legal equivalent of gross negligence manslaughter.

### The Disputed Toxicology

French investigators reported that Henri Paul's blood contained alcohol at 175–187 mg per 100 ml — more than **three times** the French legal limit of 50 mg per 100 ml. They also found traces of prescription antidepressants (fluoxetine and tiapride, medications used for alcohol dependency treatment) in his system.

However, this was immediately contested:
- Multiple colleagues who observed Paul that evening stated he appeared completely sober
- Pathologists hired by the Al-Fayed family disputed whether the blood samples were properly handled and whether contamination had occurred
- Questions about the chain of custody of the blood samples were never fully resolved to the satisfaction of all investigators
- The French investigation dossier that might definitively answer these questions is sealed until 2082

## Background

### Diana's Life and Her Unique Position

Diana Frances Spencer was born July 1, 1961, at Park House, Sandringham, the daughter of John Spencer, Viscount Althorp. She married Charles, Prince of Wales, in July 1981 in a ceremony watched by an estimated 750 million television viewers worldwide. The couple had two sons: Prince William (born 1982) and Prince Harry (born 1984).

By the mid-1980s the marriage was in serious crisis, with both parties involved in affairs. Diana gave a landmark BBC Panorama interview in November 1995, watched by 22 million viewers in Britain, in which she described the marriage as "crowded" — a reference to Charles's long relationship with Camilla Parker Bowles. Charles and Diana divorced in August 1996.

In the final years of her life, Diana became internationally prominent for humanitarian causes — particularly her landmine awareness campaign. She walked through active minefields in Angola and Bosnia in 1997, and her work directly contributed to the Ottawa Treaty banning landmines, which won the Nobel Peace Prize that year (posthumously awarded to the campaign she championed).

### What Made Her Dangerous to the Establishment

Diana's position as the most famous woman in the world, combined with her divorce from the Royal Family and her demonstrated willingness to speak publicly about uncomfortable truths, made her uniquely dangerous to powerful interests.

By 1997 she had:
- Given the unauthorized Panorama interview, humiliating the Palace
- Championed causes that were actively opposed by the British government (landmines, AIDS)
- Begun a relationship with Dodi Fayed — Egyptian, Muslim, wealthy — who his father claims she was planning to marry
- According to Mohamed Al-Fayed's claims, become pregnant with Dodi's child
- Accumulated extensive knowledge of the British establishment through years of intimate proximity to its inner workings

Diana's charitable work gave her access to children's hospitals, care homes, and abuse survivors across Britain. The extent of what she may have been told by survivors, caregivers, or whistleblowers in that context is not fully documented, but her proximity to the establishment figures implicated in the Westminster pedophile scandals is significant.

### Diana and Jimmy Savile

In 1983, Diana and Prince Charles attended the opening of a new spinal injuries center at Stoke Mandeville Hospital — where Jimmy Savile, who would later be exposed as Britain's most prolific serial sex offender with hundreds of child victims, was a prominent fundraiser. Savile operated freely at Stoke Mandeville, using it as a hunting ground for victims.

According to investigators and documented correspondence, Jimmy Savile maintained a close relationship with Prince Charles over approximately two decades. Charles sent Savile presents on special occasions and reportedly valued Savile's opinions so highly that Savile served as an informal marriage counselor during the Diana years. Savile had access to Buckingham Palace and reportedly visited regularly.

Diana would have been well aware of Savile's relationship with Charles. What she may have known about Savile's actual activities — especially as those activities were known to others in establishment circles — is a significant open question. Multiple investigators have documented that Savile's abuse was known to or suspected by numerous people in positions of authority who said nothing.

### Diana and the Westminster Pedophile Scandal

During the years of Diana's marriage and post-divorce public life, the Westminster pedophile dossier scandal was an active and partly suppressed story. In 1983, Conservative MP Geoffrey Dickens handed Home Secretary Leon Brittan a dossier containing allegations against named establishment figures. The dossier disappeared. Tom Watson MP raised it again in Parliament in 2012, calling for police to investigate "a powerful pedophile network linked to Parliament and Number 10."

Diana's divorce-era circle included individuals with knowledge of these networks. Her lawyers, advisors, charity contacts, and social world overlapped with the establishment world in which these allegations were circulating. Precisely what she knew, who she spoke to, and whether she was accumulating any form of documentary evidence is unknown — because she died in 1997, and no one has been able to ask her.

## The Claimed Connection to Child Trafficking / Pedophile Exposure

No documentary evidence has emerged from formal investigations linking Diana's death to child trafficking exposure. The trafficking-exposure theory circulates primarily in alternative media, and the more formally documented murder theories focus on her relationship with Dodi Fayed and her alleged pregnancy.

However, the circumstantial context is substantial:

- Diana had personal proximity to Jimmy Savile, the most prolific child abuser in British history, through his relationship with Prince Charles
- Diana was moving through the world of British charities and children's services throughout the 1980s and 1990s, giving her extensive contact with the institutions where abuse was occurring
- The Westminster pedophile scandal — involving named senior politicians and establishment figures — was active during the years of her public life
- Prince Charles's household had a documented close relationship with Savile, and Diana would have had reason to investigate the nature of that relationship
- Diana had demonstrated a pattern of speaking out about things the establishment wanted kept quiet

Mohamed Al-Fayed's formal murder allegations did not center on trafficking exposure — they centered on the Royal Family's alleged objection to her relationship with Dodi. But the two are not mutually exclusive. An establishment with reason to fear Diana's knowledge of multiple subjects would have had multiple reasons to act.

## Why This Death Raises Questions

- **Diana predicted her own death in writing, twice.** In October 1995, Diana told her solicitor Lord Victor Mishcon that "reliable sources" had informed her that efforts would be made to "get rid of her" or injure her in a car accident involving "brake failure." Mishcon prepared a contemporaneous note. In October 1996, Diana wrote a separate letter to her butler Paul Burrell stating: "This particular phase in my life is the most dangerous... [a specific person] is planning 'an accident' in my car, brake failure and serious head injury in order to make the path clear for him to marry Tiggy." She named a specific method and a specific motive. The crash that killed her involved a car — not brake failure, but a car crash with a head injury. The Mishcon note was locked in a Metropolitan Police safe for **six years** before being handed to the coroner.

- **Henri Paul's blood alcohol results were disputed.** Colleagues who saw him that evening said he appeared completely sober. A pathologist hired by Al-Fayed disputed the blood sample chain of custody. The questions about contamination were never definitively resolved. Paul's reported alcohol level would have made him obviously impaired — yet multiple witnesses said he appeared normal.

- **A former MI6 officer gave a sworn statement alleging intelligence service involvement.** Richard Tomlinson, a former MI6 officer, swore in a statement that he believed British intelligence was involved in the crash. He later told the inquest he "may have misremembered" and could not confirm that Henri Paul was a British agent. However, Operation Paget did confirm that Paul was likely a low-level informant for French intelligence services and police — meaning the driver of the car in which Diana died had intelligence service connections. Tomlinson's original sworn statement remains on record.

- **Fourteen surveillance cameras in the tunnel captured nothing.** Paris in 1997 had extensive CCTV coverage. The Pont de l'Alma tunnel had at least 14 cameras. According to investigators, not a single camera captured footage of the crash. The room commander on duty that night told investigators that cameras appeared to be showing only a "blurred yellow light" when staff tried to view the crash site.

- **A white Fiat Uno made contact with Diana's car — and was never found.** Paint transfer evidence and eyewitness accounts confirmed that a white Fiat Uno was present in the tunnel and made contact with Diana's Mercedes before the crash. Despite extensive investigation, the Fiat Uno and its driver were never identified. French and British investigators described this as a lasting "frustration." This mystery car remains unaccounted for nearly 30 years later.

- **France sealed its 6,000-page investigation dossier until 2082.** The French investigation produced approximately 6,000 pages of documents, including 200 witness statements, toxicology reports, previously unseen crash-scene photographs, and forensic evidence. Under French law, these are sealed until 2082 — at the earliest. Sources say French authorities could extend the restriction indefinitely. In 2007, French authorities initially claimed the entire dossier had been "lost" — just weeks before the British inquest began. Crash-scene photographs also reportedly disappeared. Evidence being "lost" and then sealed for 85 years is not a normal feature of accidental traffic deaths.

- **The 2008 inquest jury returned a verdict of "unlawful killing."** This is not the same as finding an accident. The jury found that Diana was unlawfully killed — that the deaths resulted from grossly negligent conduct, not from a simple accident. Two jurors voted differently from the majority, meaning two of eleven people who heard all the evidence were not persuaded even by the unlawful-killing finding.

- **Diana's bodyguard was changed shortly before the crash.** In the period leading up to the Paris trip, Diana and Dodi had made changes to their security arrangements. Henri Paul, who was not originally scheduled to drive, ended up at the wheel.

- **The Mishcon note was suppressed for six years.** Diana's own attorney prepared a contemporaneous note documenting Diana's fears of a staged car crash. That note was given to the Metropolitan Police Commissioner within weeks of the crash — September 18, 1997. It was locked in a safe. It was not disclosed to the coroner for six years. This is not normal handling of evidence potentially relevant to the cause of a death.

- **Mohamed Al-Fayed's formal allegations were investigated but not disproven.** Operation Paget investigated 175 specific conspiracy claims made by Al-Fayed over two years, conducting interviews with 300 witnesses and reviewing 25,000 documents. Its conclusion was that the claims were "without foundation." However, "without foundation" is not "proven false." The investigation could not explain the missing Fiat Uno, the non-functioning cameras, or certain contested aspects of the toxicology.

- **Prince Harry has publicly stated he believes his mother was murdered.** While not making formal legal allegations, Prince Harry has made statements indicating he does not fully accept the official accident narrative.

## The Counterargument

Operation Paget was a two-year investigation by 14 Metropolitan Police officers, producing an 832-page report reviewing 175 conspiracy claims. Its conclusion was unambiguous: no evidence of MI6 involvement, no evidence of an organized conspiracy. The 2008 inquest — six months of public testimony — returned the unlawful killing verdict but attributed responsibility to the intoxicated driver and pursuing paparazzi.

Henri Paul's impairment was confirmed by multiple independent forensic analyses. The Mercedes was traveling at excessive speed in a narrow tunnel while being pursued. Diana was not wearing a seatbelt — crash reconstruction experts confirmed this was a significant factor in her fatal injuries. Trevor Rees-Jones, the sole survivor, was wearing his seatbelt and has not alleged intelligence involvement.

The trafficking-exposure theory specifically has no documentary support. No formal investigation, witness, or document has connected Diana's death to child trafficking exposure. That claim circulates anonymously online without named sources or evidence.

Vicky Tomlinson later retracted or qualified the sworn statement, saying he may have misremembered.

The sealed French dossier, while obviously suspicious-looking, is technically required under French law for sensitive national documents compiled in that period — the 75-year rule applied from 2007 creates the 2082 deadline.
