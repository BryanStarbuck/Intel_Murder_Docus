## Overview

In July 1998, Marcel Vervloesem of the Belgian activist group **Werkgroep Morkhoven** handed Belgian and Dutch police a set of CD-ROMs containing what he said were tens of thousands of photographs and videos of child sexual abuse, including images of infants. The material had been traced to an address in **Zandvoort**, a seaside town on the Dutch North Sea coast. The Dutch press named the case the "Zandvoort file"; in France and Belgium it became known as *le fichier de la honte* — the file of shame.

According to Werkgroep Morkhoven's account, the first CD-ROM came from **Gerrit Ulrich**, a German man living in the Netherlands who administered a site distributing the material. Ulrich was shot dead in **Pisa, Italy, in June 1998** — before the material became public. The Dutch authorities sought the extradition of a Belgian suspect, Robert van der Plancken, then 24, over the killing, which was reported at the time as a dispute within the network rather than a silencing. Vervloesem stated that he subsequently recovered roughly twenty further CD-ROMs from Ulrich's apartment.

Werkgroep Morkhoven sent copies of the material to Interpol and, according to the group, to European heads of state. Dutch police examined the collection and reported that they were unable to identify most of the children or to establish that an organized network existed. No principal of the alleged network was convicted. Vervloesem was later prosecuted in Belgium on child sex abuse and child pornography charges and convicted in 2008 — an outcome his supporters describe as retaliation and his critics describe as an unrelated prosecution on its own evidence. Both readings remain contested.

## Why It Belongs in This Investigation

The Zandvoort case is not connected to Jeffrey Epstein by any documented person, flight log, or financial record. It is documented here because it is one of the clearest European examples of the same structure the Epstein case exposed in the United States — and because two people who investigated it died.

* Industrial-scale production and distribution of child abuse material, with an alleged international client list
* Allegations that the clients included people with political and commercial influence
* An investigation that closed without identifying the network, in the face of physical evidence that plainly existed
* The whistleblower discredited and then prosecuted
* Witness deaths, including [Gina Pardaens](../../Details/Gina_Pardaens.md), who was investigating this material when she died
* Direct overlap with the [Dutroux Affair](dutroux_affair.md) in Belgium — the same activists, the same period, the same allegations of protection reaching into government

## The Manuel Schadwald File

[Manuel Schadwald](../../Details/Manuel_Schadwald.mdx) was a twelve-year-old Berlin boy who disappeared on **24 July 1993** and has never been found. Leads pursued by Dutch and Belgian journalists and activists placed him in brothels in **Amsterdam and Rotterdam** in 1993–1994, and later in material connected to Zandvoort.

According to reporting summarized on the Wikipedia entry for the case, one of Ulrich's associates told journalists that he had brought Schadwald from Berlin to the Netherlands. The most serious allegation attached to the case — that the boy was killed aboard a sailing yacht named **Apollo** while the killing was filmed, and that the vessel was afterwards cleaned at a Dutch naval port — rests on claimed Dutch intelligence material and on witness accounts relayed by activists. **It has never been confirmed by any court, inquest, or published official document, and the Dutch and German authorities have never substantiated it.** Berlin police denied a 1997 Dutch television report that Schadwald had been identified in an abuse film. The Berlin investigation was closed in 1998 with no body, no charges, and no finding.

## The Counterargument

* Dutch police stated they reviewed the material and could not identify an organized network or most of the children depicted; investigators described the Schadwald links as circumstantial
* Much of what is claimed about the network — the yacht Apollo, the intelligence files, the client list of influential people — comes from activists and from a small number of witnesses, not from court records or published official documents
* Gerrit Ulrich's killing was investigated at the time as a dispute between individuals, with a named suspect sought for extradition, rather than as a silencing
* Marcel Vervloesem was convicted in a Belgian court in 2008; that conviction stands, whatever its origin, and it is a legitimate reason for caution about material sourced solely to him
* Several online sources that carry the Zandvoort story most aggressively are advocacy sites with no editorial standards, and the story has been repeatedly embellished as it has circulated
