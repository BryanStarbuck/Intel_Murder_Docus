## Overview

North Fox Island is a 428-acre island in Lake Michigan, 15–17 miles offshore from Leelanau County, Michigan — remote, accessible only by boat or small aircraft, and, beginning around 1975, the primary production site for one of the most thoroughly documented child exploitation operations in American history. 

Francis Duffield Shelden, a wealthy Grosse Pointe businessman who purchased the island in 1960 for approximately $20,000, built a private airstrip and cabins, incorporated a fraudulent charitable nonprofit, and ran a subscription business in which dozens to hundreds of boys — recruited from underprivileged backgrounds with promises of outdoor mentorship — were filmed in sex acts and the footage distributed to paying clients nationwide. The wealthiest subscribers could visit the island in person.

The operation was exposed when Shelden's street-level recruiter, Gerald Richards, was arrested in July 1976. But by the time federal arrest warrants were finally issued more than four months later — delays investigators later attributed to a county prosecutor with documented personal connections to at least one figure who had personally visited the island — Shelden had fled to France, then Amsterdam. He died there in 1996 with an active U.S. arrest warrant, never prosecuted.

## The Island

North Fox Island (approximately 2 miles long, 1 mile wide) sits in northern Lake Michigan. Its isolation was operationally essential — no permanent residents, no public presence, no oversight. Shelden's private airstrip became the primary access point. Boys were transported in his Piper Seneca II aircraft. On the island, Shelden built cabins and trails and developed what he presented to parents as an outdoor youth program. Parents paid approximately $85 per six-day stay, lending a veneer of legitimacy that masked what was occurring.

## Front Organizations

To launder the operation as legitimate charity work — and to capture federal, state, and county payments for "serving" troubled youth — Shelden and associates built a network of nonprofit fronts:

| Organization | Role |
|---|---|
| **Brother Paul's Children's Mission** | Primary front; incorporated June 18, 1975 in Michigan; operated the island program; received government per-child payments |
| **Church of the New Revelation** (Kearny, NJ) | Parent organization; president was Adam Starchild (Malcolm McConahy) |
| **Educational Foundation for Youth** (Chicago, IL) | Affiliated front |
| **Ocean Living Institute** (New Jersey) | Claimed focus on underwater habitats and aquaculture; alleged tax shelter |

Federal, state, and county governments were billed for the boys: counties paid up to $150/month per child, the state up to $400, and the federal government up to $700 — meaning public funds were funneled directly into the operation.

## Key Figures

### Francis Duffield Shelden (1928–1996)

Shelden was born September 5, 1928, into one of Detroit's founding industrial dynasties — a descendant of Russell A. Alger, 20th Governor of Michigan, U.S. Senator, and Secretary of War under President McKinley. He attended Millbrook Prep School, graduated from Yale University in 1950, and earned a Master's in Geology from Wayne State University. He served in the Michigan Air National Guard and built his personal wealth through real estate and oil investments. He volunteered as a Big Brothers mentor through the Ann Arbor YMCA and served on the Cranbrook boys' school board — both positions that gave him access to boys and institutional credibility.

When Richards was arrested in July 1976, someone warned Shelden. Police who subsequently raided his Ann Arbor home found all filing cabinets emptied. He fled to France, married a French citizen to obtain citizenship, and relocated to Amsterdam. While a fugitive, he contributed articles to *P.A.N. (Paedophile Action News)*, a publication of the Paedophile Information Exchange, beginning February 1978, operating openly in Amsterdam's organized pro-pedophilia circles.

Shelden died July 9, 1996, in Amsterdam at age 68 — with an active U.S. federal arrest warrant outstanding. Amsterdam police searched his residence after his death. A death certificate was obtained by researchers from Dutch records. No criminal trial ever took place.

### Gerald Stewart Richards (1941–1994 or 1998, disputed)

A physical education teacher at St. Joseph's Catholic School in Port Huron, Richards also worked at an adult bookstore and performed as a magician at children's birthday parties. He connected with Shelden through a coded classified advertisement in *Better Life Monthly*, an underground pedophile-oriented newsletter. His naturopath's office in Port Huron was used as a filming location.

Richards was the primary street-level recruiter. He reportedly told investigators: *"It's the easiest thing in the world to get a kid. What you want is a kid with no moral, religious background."* He maintained customer files on approximately 600 individuals who received child sexual abuse material.

He was arrested July 25, 1976, after the parent of one of his students raised concerns. He pleaded guilty to child molestation, was sentenced to two-to-twenty years at Jackson Prison, and in exchange for immunity, testified before the U.S. Senate Subcommittee on Juvenile Delinquency in May–June 1977 — testimony describing the ring's structure and national customer network in detail. He was released early, re-arrested in 1988 on child pornography charges. His death date is disputed in accessible records: sources cite either 1994 or 1998.

### Dyer Grossman (b. 1941, Carmel, NY)

A millionaire who taught science at a prestigious all-boys boarding school, Grossman was listed as vice president of Brother Paul's Children's Mission and executive director of the Ocean Living Institute. He used multiple aliases — "Dale Osterman," "Elliot Crossman," "Elijah Crossman." He was charged with criminal sexual conduct against two underage boys but fled before arrest. As of available records, his whereabouts after 1976 are unknown. No credible obituary, arrest record, or confirmed death notice has been found by researchers.

### Adam Starchild (born Malcolm Willis McConahy, 1946–2006)

President of the Church of the New Revelation — the organizational parent of Brother Paul's. His criminal history predated the ring substantially: suspended as assistant scoutmaster in 1965 for admitting sexual interest in boys; convicted in 1967 of possessing obscene literature and contributing to the delinquency of a minor; convicted in 1968 of $57,000 check fraud; convicted of mail fraud (1969); forgery conviction in England followed by extradition and bail-jumping conviction in the U.S. (1974). He later adopted the name "Adam Aristotle Starchild" and marketed himself as a "perpetual traveler," offshore tax attorney, and white-collar consultant.

In 1976, Shelden transferred $2 million in securities to Starchild for an offshore trust; Starchild's subsequent mismanagement led to civil litigation (*National Bank of Detroit v. Francis D. Shelden*, 730 F.2d 421, 6th Cir. 1984). He was later convicted of mail fraud (1986), tax fraud (1989), and multiple CSAM-related offenses. He was never charged in connection with the North Fox Island operation itself. He died September 22, 2006, in Spain.

## Recruitment and Distribution Network

Boys aged approximately 7–16, predominantly from underprivileged backgrounds, were targeted with promises of mentorship and outdoor experience. The distribution network extended beyond Michigan:

- **Father Bud's Boys Farm** (Monteagle, Tennessee), operated by Rev. Cladius Vermilye
- **MAIL-O-MATIC** (North Carolina forwarding service) — for distributing CSAM by mail
- **F&S Distributions** (California)
- ***Better Life Monthly*** and ***Hermes*** newsletters — underground pedophile publications that used coded classified ads to facilitate contact between offenders and attract clients

According to Richards' Senate testimony, the network was not a local operation but part of a nationwide organized child exploitation and distribution system. His 600-name customer file represented only what police recovered; Shelden's customer records were destroyed before police arrived.

## Connection to the Oakland County Child Killer

Between February 1976 and March 1977 — overlapping with the North Fox Island investigation — four children were abducted and murdered in Oakland County, Michigan: Mark Stebbins (12), Jill Robinson (12), Kristine Mihelich (10), and Timothy King (11). The killer was never officially identified.

**Christopher Busch** was a documented client of Brother Paul's Children's Mission. The son of H. Lee Busch, a General Motors vice president, he was a convicted pedophile investigated as an OCCK suspect. Oakland County task force polygraphers administered examinations in 1977; investigators who later reviewed the results believed Busch had not passed, though at the time results were publicly described as passed. Busch died November 20, 1978, at age 27, from an apparent gunshot wound to the head ruled suicide — with a hand-drawn sketch of a bound boy found at the scene.

In 2012, Oakland County prosecutors announced that Busch's DNA did not match physical evidence from victims. This did not resolve questions about whether he played an indirect role in the crimes. No forensic or prosecutorial finding has established that Shelden, Richards, or the ring's principals committed the OCCK murders.

Catherine Broad — the sister of Timothy King, the fourth OCCK victim — has documented the overlap between the North Fox Island investigation and the OCCK case in her investigative blog, and has published extensively on what she describes as institutional failures in both investigations.

**Arch Sloan** and **Vincent James Gunnels** were also investigated in the OCCK case: DNA from hairs linked to victims matched Sloan's vehicle passengers, and Gunnels failed a 2009 polygraph on questions about Kristine Mihelich's killing. The degree to which either was connected to the North Fox Island network is not established in publicly available records.

## Cover-Up Indicators

The following anomalies are documented in investigative records and court filings — not speculation:

1. **Warrant delay by Washtenaw County Prosecutor Peter Deegan:** Richards was arrested July 25, 1976. Deegan attempted to delay the search warrant for Shelden's property on July 28. He deferred the first arrest warrant request on August 2. He delayed further requests in early September. On October 8, case complaints against Shelden and Grossman were "closed in error" in Deegan's office. Arrest warrants were finally issued December 7, 1976 — more than four months after Richards' arrest. Both Shelden and Grossman had fled.

2. **Personal connection to a figure who had visited the island:** Charlevoix District Attorney Don Berlage — a former FBI agent and Big Brothers program director — had visited North Fox Island with his two teenage sons at least once. Berlage and Deegan reportedly shared a mutual acquaintance. The nature of any relationship between Deegan's delays and Berlage's island visit has not been established by any official investigation.

3. **Evidence destruction:** When police finally executed the search warrant on Shelden's Ann Arbor home, all filing cabinets had been emptied. Someone warned Shelden before police could secure the location.

4. **600 customer names never fully prosecuted:** Richards' files contained approximately 600 individuals who received CSAM. The majority were never identified or charged.

5. **FBI files withheld:** As of 2026, more than 1,047 FBI records related to the case remain unreleased. Documentary filmmaker Colin Browen filed FOIA requests and subsequently sued the FBI after officials stated the process would take "more than six years." The lawsuit is ongoing.

6. **Alleged FBI flood destruction:** According to researcher Catherine Broad's FOIA responses, the FBI claimed that some Busch/Shelden files were destroyed in a catastrophic flood.

7. **Only the lowest-status participant convicted and served time:** Gerald Richards — a gym teacher without resources to flee — was the only member of the core North Fox Island operation to face substantial criminal justice consequences. The wealthy operators and their clients faced virtually none.

## Connected to the Franklin Scandal

The North Fox Island ring's operational model — recruiting vulnerable boys, conditioning them through abuse, creating CSAM for distribution to wealthy subscribers, using fraudulent nonprofits, and achieving near-total impunity for principal operators — mirrors the structure documented a decade later in the [Franklin Child Prostitution Ring](Franklin_Child_Prostitution_Rin.md) in Nebraska. Richards' 1977 Senate testimony explicitly described how networks like North Fox Island "not only recruited children as product but also fostered and communicated to the massive customer base" — suggesting an interconnected national infrastructure.

Some researchers allege a continuous network linking North Fox Island through Franklin to other rings. These connections are alleged by survivor testimony and researchers; they are not established by judicial findings.
